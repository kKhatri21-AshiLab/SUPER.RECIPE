Have you ever said “I know this is wrong… but I still want to do it”?

If yes — welcome to the world of rationality and irrationality.



Today, we’re answering a simple question:

What does it mean to be rational?

And why do humans so often act against their own logic?


Imagine this:

You’ve been eating healthy, exercising, and losing weight.

You’re out with friends…

And then you see a big slice of strawberry cheesecake

Your brain says: “Don’t do it.”

Your desire says: “Just this once.”

That uncomfortable feeling?

That’s inner conflict.


Now another situation.

You have a small surgery scheduled on Friday the 13th.

You’re not superstitious…

But still, you feel like rescheduling.

You know it’s illogical —

Yet the feeling doesn’t go away.

[Philosophy Background ]

This struggle isn’t new.

Ancient philosophers talked about reason thousands of years ago.

• Plato said reason should control emotions and desires.

• Aristotle believed only humans can truly reason.

• Descartes said the mind uses reason to control the body.

For centuries, reason was seen as what makes humans special.



Later, economist Adam Smith claimed humans always act rationally and in self-interest.

But modern psychology proved something shocking:

Humans often know the right thing… and still do the wrong thing.



There are two kinds of rationality:

1️⃣ Epistemic Rationality

Having correct beliefs based on logic and evidence.

2️⃣ Instrumental Rationality

Actually acting on those correct beliefs.

You need both to be truly rational.


So when logic and desire clash —

You’re not broken.

You’re human.
